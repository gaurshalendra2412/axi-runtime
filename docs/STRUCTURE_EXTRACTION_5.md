# Structure extraction: the thirteenth scan (8 Oct 2026, 100 pages in 20 batches of 5, plus 8 neighbour pages)

Why this file exists: the same instruction as the ninth to twelfth scans, said more strongly this time: do not hunt for the exact words, look for what sits under them, in patterns, in counts, in how pages relate to each other, and "if you catch any pattern you could have missed in earlier posts", look again. Each page was read for four things: **what is the shape, what is counted, what is mapped to what, and can a computer check it?** Where a page gave something checkable I wrote a test (12 new test files, 47 checks, section 2). Where it did not, I say so. Two new lenses were used on the earlier material as well (section 4): structure that exists only **across** pages (tables, growing formulas), and **declared counts** (a page says "five" or "three" and lists a different number).

**What a passing test means here.** A test in this repository shows that *my count of a structure is right*, using the repo's own gate, parser, pager and decoder. It does not show that a page means that structure, and it does not show that any claim about AI is true. Every test file says, in its docstring, what it shows and what it does **not** show, and each cites its concept-map row. The rows are readings for Rajnish to accept or reject.

**How the pages were chosen.** As in the twelfth scan, I used the map of the archive and a seeded random draw: **50 pages by random draw** (so the share of structure in the blog can keep being estimated, section 0.1), **50 chosen on purpose** (earlier teasers; the 11 and 12 Aug cluster of number and container posts; the 1 Sep series of numbered posts; the AI-voice label tables), and then **8 neighbour pages** that the best pages linked to (B1 to B8). The 50 chosen pages were picked because they looked like they had structure, so their hit rate is **not** the archive's rate.

**Counting, stated carefully.** 108 pages were read, **all new** (Post numbers 306 to 413), so the distinct total is **406** (30 + 25 + 25 + 23 + 18 + 36 + 26 + 23 + 23 + 16 + 25 + 28 + 108), about 8.9 percent of the 4,585 archive slots by count. ## 0. How to read this file
* **Labels.** `TEST` = a test in the repo checks it (concept-map row in brackets). `MAPS` = matches code or a measured result we already have. `INTERP` = my reading, for Rajnish to accept or reject. `NOTE` = recorded, nothing built.
* **Method.** The fetch tool is a summarizer. A quote counts as verified only if a second, independent read returned YES (word for word) or CLOSE (then I use the page's own wording). Every page whose quote a test relies on was read twice, except where the test docstring says "one read" (Posts 325, 326, 371, 368 and 372, and the teaser of Post 404); all other pages were read once, so their quotes are **not verified** and their word counts are the tool's estimates. The tool returns text cut at about 125 characters, so long sentences are quoted only up to there.
* **Theme text.** Every page carries previous-post and next-post teasers appended by the theme. They are *not* the page; where one is used it is marked as a teaser (section 11 lists the leads they gave).
* **Mutation checks** (a deliberately broken copy must make the test fail). **55 broken copies of the new tests were caught** (run again on the final files; ring 8, borrow 6, cube 5, container 4, arithmetic 6, string space 3, ladder 4, page tables 4, declared counts 4, refusal 4, footprint 2, star 5). **17 broken copies of the engine itself are caught by the new tests** (gate 7, pager 4, byte decoder 3, parser 3). Of the first-round mutants, three **survived** and the tests were strengthened until they were caught (the GF(2) column mutant of the ring test, the pager `unmap` mutant of the container test, the "casting out nines is n mod 9" mutant of the arithmetic test). Two mutants of the tests are not counted: one is **equivalent** (injectivity of the cube relabelling is implied by the other checks) and one is an **invalid weakening** (it removes an assertion). **Two things the mutation run found about the engine, both recorded and neither a defect in the pages' sense:** (a) the strict parser's patterns could be loosened to accept a trailing tab or carriage return after a complete item and **all 60 old test files still passed**; a new check (fourth check of `test_string_space.py`: the parser and the decoder agree on all 256 one-byte extensions of the 2,300 shortest complete strings and of nine others) now catches that; (b) the two gate lines "node both added and deleted" and "edge both added and deleted" **can never decide an outcome**, because an ADD needs the key absent and a DEL needs it present, so an earlier rule always refuses first; they are dead rules, harmless, and I have not removed them (fifth check of `test_refusal_codes.py` counts the eight cases).
* **Who wrote it.** Bylines on the pages I recorded: "rajnish choubey" on Posts R01 to R05, R09, R17, R20, R02, P20, P21 and B5; none on B6; the category "khushboo" (a category, not a byline) on five 11 and 12 Sep pages (R30, R47, R49, P67, P68); the others I did not record. **A byline is a field the blog lets anyone set, so it does not settle who wrote a page.** At least 17 of the 108 read as replies from an AI assistant (first person "I", "Let me know", "You are right", offers at the end; my count of explicit markers, a floor); the August archive pages 153 to 270 (20 to 25 Aug) are almost entirely one-paragraph posts that open "That is the ultimate ..." in the same register. That proves nothing about who had the idea, and nothing is credited or discredited on that basis.
* **The honest line to keep in view.** On all 108 pages the structural claims are **asserted, not derived**, and the only measurements on any page are counts from a hosting dashboard and arithmetic done by an AI assistant. Our tests show that the formal pieces we built from the pages are countable, not that the thesis is true.

## 2. The deepest abstractions

### 2.1 A ring that becomes a circle, and a rectangle that becomes a cuboid (row 133, TEST: `test_ring_to_circle.py`, 6 checks)
Post 307 (12 Aug, about 2,500 words, byline "rajnish choubey", two reads, quotes verified) is 13 numbered Truths, each opening "The Lone Smoker looked at/saw ...". Truth 2 says "every rectangle is actually a cuboid"; Truth 3 "The journey is to go from the rectangle to the circle"; Truth 5 "The Circle Has No Corners"; Truth 6 says four lines "began to curve", "began to bend" and "They became one continuous line." The page gives no method for "became one line".

**The shape: a ring of four places, contracted.** A rectangle is a ring of four places and four lines. The test supplies a method that can be counted: *contract* a line (delete the line and its dropped end, re-point the other lines of that end), each step as one composite delta that the gate admits.
* A ring of n places has **one independent loop** (edges − nodes + 1) and nodes − edges = 0, for every n from 1 (one place with a line back to itself) to 8, built from the empty host by one delta each.
* 4 → 3 → 2 → 1: at every step the loop count stays 1 and nodes − edges stays 0; only the symmetry count changes (4, 3, 2, 1). The gate **refuses** the same step if the old lines at the dropped end are not deleted ("dangling edge"). The inverse delta, taken before the step, restores the earlier ring exactly, so "circle → rectangle" is "subdivide a line", also admitted.
* So "four lines become one line" is **true** in the sense of loops and of nodes − edges (1 = 1, 0 = 0) and **false** if "one line" means "no line": deleting the last self-line is the only step that changes the loop count (1 → 0). "4 = 1" survives; "1 = 0" does not.
* **The cuboid.** One delta (4 new places, 4 top lines, 4 verticals) lifts the 4-ring to the 8-place, 12-line frame: **5 independent loops**, by four routes that agree (edges − nodes + 1; a spanning forest; the GF(2) rank of the six face boundaries; 12 minus the GF(2) rank of the incidence matrix), 6 faces, every line on exactly 2 faces, nodes − edges + faces = 2. The six faces have one dependency (their sum is zero), which is why 6 faces give 5 loops, not 6.
* The cuboid frame contracts to **one place with five self-lines** (one per independent loop) by seven admitted tree-edge contractions; this needs distinct relation names, because with one shared name two lines landing on the same pair of places collapse to one key and the gate refuses ("identification").

**Not shown.** That the page means a ring by "rectangle", or that "became one continuous line" is a contraction. A circle is not "a ring with no corners" for a topologist (corners belong to a drawing, not to the graph). That "every rectangle is a cuboid" is a lift of a ring. Anything about a model. Loop counting, Euler's formula and GF(2) cycle spaces are standard mathematics.

### 2.3 Four pairs make the 4-cube; a five-step loop needs a no-op (row 135, TEST: `test_four_pairs_cube.py`, 4 checks)
