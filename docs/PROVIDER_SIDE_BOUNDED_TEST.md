## What the second run left open
1. **Line 2 read NO.** One reply of the exact-state arm (`S`) ran to the 96-token limit inside a name (job 19, step 12; the same reply in the 32-job and the 64-job cell). The expression of that run lets a relation name be any number of letters, digits and underscores, and greedy decoding looped inside it. By that page's own rule, line 2 NO means "neither is claimed": neither the plain time ratios nor the exact-steps-per-hour figures were claimed from that run, and the Result said that the clean way to clear it is to run `S` again with a name of bounded length.
2. **The Result's check afterwards was not a run.** It counted the cut-off step, and then all 19 wrong steps of job 19, as exact, and found that the ratios would move from 39.5 and 49.3 to at most 40.3 and 49.8. That was arithmetic done after the numbers were seen. This test replaces it with a run under a rule written first.
3. **What the cap cannot do, said before anything else.** It does not make job 19 right. At step 12 the oracle adds the node `(3,0)` (`ADD[3,0:4]`); the model writes an edge from and to that node instead, and the gate refuses an edge whose end is not in the graph, whatever its name is. The state does not move, the job stays behind, and it will stay behind. So the exact share of `S` (88.3, 94.6 and 97.2 percent in the second run) is expected to be **the same**, and the two lines that ask for more exactness (12 and 13 below, carried over from the second run) are expected to read NO again. The cap clears line 2, and the figures that line was holding back; it does not clear anything else.

## What changes and what does not
**Does not change:** the model (`Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ`), the engine (vLLM 0.31.0), the GPU assumed (a Colab T4), the cache fixed at **80,000 tokens**, `max_model_len` 8,192, greedy decoding, at most 96 new tokens, prefix caching on, the 30-step jobs with episode e at seed 20261017 x 1000 + e, the same prompts, policy `repair_m6`, the lockstep rounds, the stop rule at 7,600 tokens of a kept chat, the runner and its checkpoint, the measures (including exact steps per GPU hour: 3,600 times the steps after which the held state equals the oracle's, over the wall seconds of the cell). The two modules above are pinned by their sha256 in the test: `provider_side.py` `bfb4734e171d9244cd47d10402be46528a557095a8275d02b38481a0e2b0b1d3`, `provider_side_constrained.py` `03452148e28c9f31372f12cc11ef1808ac67308adff972fa19d4aeeef7c96f7d`.
**Changes:**
* **The length of a name is at most 16 characters.** Every reply of both arms must match the expression below. It is the second run's expression with `[A-Za-z0-9_]+` replaced by `[A-Za-z0-9_]{1,16}` and nothing else; it is inside the second run's language. Its sha256 is `6f4e5a993f26ccd471bff13422fa622b7820858a91e05d43c2bccfbe6b864245` (the second run's was `21cdc0bf7824cff3866413dd74fc5939ccc01135e3b241dfcf9fbb1440bb0ec9`). `tests/test_provider_side_bounded.py` pins it and checks the expression against the repository's strict parser and grammar on random and on mutated strings.

* **The cells are four:** `S` at 8, 32 and 64 jobs, and `KC` at 8 jobs, in this order: `S` 8, `KC` 8, `S` 32, `S` 64. About ten minutes of GPU. **The kept chat at 32 and 64 jobs is not run again** (1 h 19 min and 2 h 52 min of GPU in the second run). Its two cells are read from the second run's merged file, `docs/results/axi_provider_constrained.json` (sha256 `7f54c3dd37fd4dcf61e8a7991af285d6ad1a0d691362496216949a26b45b819d`, pinned in the test).
* **Why that is allowed, and what it does not show.** With greedy decoding, a reply whose every token the narrower grammar allows is the same reply (the wider grammar offered the engine more tokens and it chose one that the narrower still offers). Every reply of the two `KC` cells is inside the new language (checked on the saved replies, below), so the cap would not have changed one of them. That is an argument and a check on data, not a run. What it leaves open is that the comparison of `S` (new session) with `KC` at 32 and 64 jobs (old sessions) mixes Colab sessions. Lines 7 and 8 are the check on that: they compare the new session's speed with the second run's for both arms, and the rule below says what the Result may quote if they read NO.
* **A replay check is part of the test.** Every reply of every new cell is compared with the reply of the same job at the same step in the second run (lines 3 and 4), and the reply that looped is looked at on its own (line 5).
* **The reading lines are 13** (below). The headline is the exact steps per GPU hour at the lightest load (line 9) and at the heaviest (line 11).

## Predictions (mine, written first)

## Reading rules (fixed now; the printout prints exactly these)
The thirteen lines above are the reading rules; `provider_side_bounded.verdict_bounded` computes them from this run's cells and the second run's file. Line 1 uses every cell of this run; line 2 every cell of this run (the share of replies the strict parser accepted, and the replies that used all 96 new tokens); line 3 every `S` cell, step by step against the second run's cell of the same arm and K (the same text for the same job at the same step, except the steps of job 19 from step 12 on; and the same number of steps in every job); line 4 the `KC` cell at 8 jobs against the second run's; line 5 the reply at step 12 of job 19 in the cells that hold that job (`S` at 32 and at 64 jobs); line 6 the exact share of each `S` cell against the second run's (within 0.005); lines 7 and 8 the seconds per step of each `S` cell and of the `KC` cell at 8 jobs against the second run's (within 30 percent either way); lines 9, 10 and 11 the exact steps per GPU hour at K = 8 (both cells of this run), 32 and 64 (`S` of this run against `KC` of the second run); line 12 every K at which both arms are held; line 13 every `S` cell. Definitions as on the second page: "accepted by the strict parser" is `strict_parse(text) is not None`; "admitted by the gate" is that the parsed reply passes the gate against the state the job held; "exact steps per GPU hour" is 3,600 times the steps after which the held state equals the oracle's, over the wall seconds of the cell. Thresholds as in the code: line 2: 99.5 percent parsed, no reply with 96 new tokens; line 5: fewer than 96 new tokens; line 6: 0.005; lines 7 and 8: 0.30; line 9: 2 times; lines 10 and 11: 10 times each; line 12: 0.05; line 13: 0.98. A line that needs a cell the run does not hold reads "not run".

**The rule that decides what the Result may claim.** Lines 2 and 3 both read yes: the exact-steps-per-hour lines (9 to 11) are claimed as read, with the exactness of both arms written next to every figure. The point ratios of lines 10 and 11 (the "x times") are quoted as numbers only if lines 7 and 8 also read yes; otherwise they are quoted only as "at least 10 times" and the speed of both sessions is written beside them. The plain time ratios are claimed as ratios at equal exactness only if line 12 reads yes. Line 2 or 3 reads NO: no claim is made from this run until the cause is found.

**How the result will be worded.** The sentence the Result may use is *N exact steps per GPU hour against M, at these exactness levels, on this setup*: in GPU seconds on the machine the run had, with the engine version and the GPU written next to them, with the exactness of both arms in the same sentence, and with the note that the kept chat at 32 and 64 jobs was measured in the second run. Never a multiple of capacity ("N times the capacity", "N GPUs for one", "N times as many users"), never a count of GPUs saved, never dollars or a percentage of cost, never "proven" (this is one model, one GPU and one run), never a statement about any provider, and never a multiple of speed without the exactness beside it. A "NO" on a claim line is a finding about this setup and goes into the article as such.

## What this does not show
Dollars; other GPUs (the absolute seconds are a free T4's); other models, engines or cache sizes; whether the grammar makes a reply right (it makes it well formed: job 19 stays wrong); the kept chat at 32 and 64 jobs under the capped expression (read from the second run, by the argument above); a capacity in jobs per GPU (the test sends K jobs in lockstep and counts exact steps per hour; it does not find the number of jobs a GPU can hold); retries (this test sends one request a step and never asks again); traffic that is not a round of K jobs; a cache that moves rows to CPU memory or disk; replies in ordinary words; jobs longer than 30 steps; **energy (never measured; only time)**. It says nothing about the tensor-level roadmap.

## How it is run
Open `python/colab/axi_provider_side_bounded.ipynb` in Colab with a T4 and run the cells one at a time: it installs vLLM 0.31.0, connects Drive, fixes the output stream, loads the model with the capped constraint, runs the safety cell (3 jobs of 3 steps in each arm, then the probe), then the four cells in order. Each finished cell is written as `axi_provider_b_<arm>_K<k>.json` and copied to Drive. If Colab stops, reconnect, run the first cells again and the cell you were in: finished cells are skipped and a running one continues from its checkpoint. The last cell puts the four cells together, reads the second run's file from the repository, prints the table and the 13 lines, and offers the files for download. The Result goes below the line at the end of this page, and a result test will recompute every number in it from the saved files.
