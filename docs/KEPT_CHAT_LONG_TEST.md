# Test 42: the kept chat with prefix caching on, against AXI, over jobs of 1,500 steps (written BEFORE the run, 16 Nov 2026)

*The architecture is Rajnish Choubey's; the code and every measurement are the owner's, with an AI assistant as a tool. AXI is a layer on the host side: the host holds the state, the model is unchanged, and every claim here is about the state the host holds. This page is written before the harness is run on any of these cells; the Result goes below it and nothing above the Result line is edited afterwards.*

## Why this test
The question every serving team asks first: with the chat kept and the provider's prefix cache switched on, what does the exact-state arm still save, and how far does the kept chat get? The repository's own pages say the saving "may be smaller" with the cache on, and every kept-chat measurement so far is at short length (Tests 19, 21 and 35: the kept chat stops after 24 to 56 steps on an 8,192-token context; its 4 jobs together ran 181 to 187 steps). **The question: on the same 7B model, with prefix caching on, at a 32,768-token context, how many steps does a kept chat reach, what does each step cost it (computed prompt tokens, seconds), and what does the exact-state arm cost and hold over jobs of 1,500 steps?**

## What I read before writing this page
Offline, from the repository's job generator (CPU, no model), recorded here so the predictions are read knowing it:
* Jobs 192 to 231 of the sixth run's generator, asked for 1,500 steps: 33 of 40 reach 1,500 steps; the others end at 908 to 1,436 steps (the generator stops a script when no request fits). Jobs 192, 194, 196, 197 and 198 reach 1,500 steps and are the jobs of this test.
* The held state of those jobs stays between about 30 and 40 items over 1,500 steps, and the exact-state prompt stays between about 730 and 1,330 characters (a few hundred tokens): the prompt of the exact-state arm does not grow with the history.
* The kept chat of the repository (`KC`) shows the whole current state in every user message, so its history grows by about 660 characters of request and state per step. With the replies set to the oracle's, the whole chat prompt is about 7,000 tokens at step 50, 14,000 at step 100, 34,000 at step 200 and 75,000 at step 400 (characters divided by 3.2, an assumption), and about 300,000 at step 1,500. **The kept chat of the repository therefore cannot reach step 1,500 on a model with a context window of 32,768 tokens, or of 131,072 with YaRN scaling; it reaches about step 190 and about step 550.** The repository's rule stops a kept chat before its next prompt would pass a set number of tokens; this test raises that number to fit the window and changes nothing else about the arm.
* A chat that shows the state once and then only the requests (a "lean" chat) grows about 10 times more slowly but must hold the state in the model's memory; it is a different arm and is measured in the test of summary, window and retrieval memory (list item 5), not here.

## What is run
The harness of Tests 35 and 38, unchanged (`python/axi/experiments/provider_side_big.py`, repo commit `55631ec`), with these settings: model `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ`, vLLM 0.31.0, one NVIDIA L4, prefix caching on, greedy decoding, no retry, the grammar that names the coordinates, at most 96 new tokens, policy `repair_m6`. Three things are set at run time by the driver, without editing any repository file: `max_model_len` 32,768; the kept-chat stop rule's limit (`provider_side.MAX_CONTEXT`) 32,000 tokens instead of 7,600; the cache 3,000 blocks (48,000 tokens), so that a 32,000-token chat fits in it.

| cell | jobs | arm | steps asked | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `A1` | 192 | S (exact state each step) | 1,500 | one job alone: the time of a step with nothing else on the card |
| `B1` | 192 | KC (whole chat kept, cache on) | 1,500 | the same job as `A1`, run until the stop rule ends it |
| `A4` | 194, 196, 197, 198 | S | 1,500 | four jobs side by side: the exact share over 6,000 steps |
| `B2`, `B3`, `B4` | 194, 196, 197 | KC | 1,500 | three more kept chats, one at a time, each until the stop rule |

**Stage 2 (run after stage 1, if the engine accepts the setting):** the same model with static YaRN scaling (factor 4, original 32,768 positions) and `max_model_len` 131,072, the kept-chat limit at 130,000 tokens and a cache of 9,000 blocks (144,000 tokens). Cells `C1` (S arm, job 192, 1,500 steps) and `D1` (KC arm, job 192). YaRN changes the model's position handling at every length, which is why the S arm is run under the same setting. If the engine refuses the setting, stage 2 is reported as not run, with the error.

## What is measured
Per cell: steps run; the share of steps whose held state equals the oracle's, over the whole run and per window of 100 steps; steps reached before the stop; computed prompt tokens per step (prompt tokens minus tokens served from the cache); the share of prompt tokens served from the cache; seconds per step (the time of a round; in the one-job cells this is the time of one step), over the first and last 30 steps and per window of 100; the prompt length per step; the GPU board's mean power.

## Predictions (mine, written first)
Each line carries my probability that it reads "yes". A line that comes out "NO" is a finding, not an error.

1. [instrument] every stage-1 cell ran without an engine error, the S-arm jobs ran all 1,500 steps and every kept chat ended by the stop rule (85 %)
2. [claim] the S arm held the oracle's state on at least 99 percent of its steps over `A1` and `A4` together (65 %)
3. [claim] the S arm's exact share in steps 1,001 to 1,500 is within 2 points of its share in steps 1 to 500 (75 %)
4. [claim] the S arm's mean prompt tokens in its last 100 steps are at most 1.3 times those of its first 100 (90 %)
5. [claim] every kept chat ended by the stop rule after step 120 and before step 250 (85 %)
6. [claim] the kept chats held the oracle's state on at least 99 percent of the steps they ran (70 %)
7. [claim] in `B1` against `A1`, over steps 1 to 150, the kept chat computes at least 1.2 times as many prompt tokens per step (75 %)
8. [claim] the kept chat's seconds per step in its last 30 steps are at least 1.3 times those of its first 30 (50 %)
9. [claim] in `B1` against `A1`, over steps 1 to 150, the kept chat takes at least 1.3 times the seconds per step of the exact-state arm (50 %)
10. [claim] the kept chat's prompt tokens served from the cache are at least 90 percent of all its prompt tokens in `B1` (80 %)
11. [claim] the S arm's seconds per step in `A1` in steps 1,401 to 1,500 are within 15 percent of those in steps 1 to 100 (70 %)
12. [claim, stage 2] the engine accepts the YaRN setting and runs `C1` and `D1` (60 %); given that, the kept chat in `D1` reaches at least 500 steps before the stop rule ends it (50 %) and holds the oracle's state on at least 99 percent of them (45 %)

What would count against AXI here: a kept chat with the cache on that takes no more time per step than the exact-state arm (the ratio of line 9 below 1.1) and computes no more prompt tokens per step (the ratio of line 7 below 1.05) while holding the state as exactly; or an exact-state arm below 95 percent exact over the 1,500 steps. Either is reported as it is. Not counting against AXI and not for it: that the kept chat cannot reach step 1,500 in a 32,768-token window. That is the arithmetic of the chat's growth with the window set by the model; it is reported as a fact about the kept chat, not as a saving of the exact-state arm.

## Honest limits
One card (L4), one 7B quantised model, greedy decoding, one engine and its version, synthetic service graphs, one family of jobs, four to five jobs per arm (`A1` and `B1` are the same single job; the kept chats are one job each, so the kept chat's exact share rests on at most four chats of about 190 steps). The repository's kept chat re-shows the state in every message; a chat that does not is another arm (list item 5). The kept chat's limit and window are set by this test; a model with a longer window (a 1,000,000-token Qwen 7B, say) would reach further and is not run. In `A1` and `B1` the seconds per step include the engine's time to compile the named grammar of each step, the same in both arms. YaRN (stage 2) is a static scaling that the model's authors document, and it may lower the model's exactness at short length; it is not run on the S arm without the kept chat beside it. The per-step files stay on the Colab machine; a compact export of the per-step times, tokens and exactness, with the sha256 of each raw file, is saved with the Result.

None of the three conditions was met.

What this shows and what it does not. With the provider's prefix cache on, the kept chat is as exact as the exact-state arm on every step it ran (1,116 of 1,116), and the cache works as it should (98 to 99 percent of its prompt tokens served from it). It still computes 1.5 to 1.8 times the prompt tokens of the exact-state arm over the same steps, its seconds per step rise with the length of the chat (0.34 s in its first 30 steps to 1.37 s in its last 30 in the 131k window, against a flat 0.28 to 0.31 s for the exact-state arm over 1,500 steps), and it stops: after 108 to 235 steps in a 32,768-token window and after 337 steps in a 131,072-token window with YaRN, against 1,500 of 1,500 for the exact-state arm in every run. The stop is the arithmetic of the chat's growth with the window set by the model, not a saving of the exact-state arm; the saving this test can claim is in the computed tokens and the seconds per step over the same steps, and in what happens as the job goes on.

Limits specific to this run. The kept chat of the repository shows the state in every message; it is the heaviest honest version, and the lean chat that shows it once is measured in Test 44. The only single-job pair is `B1` with `A1`, and `B1` is the shortest chat (108 steps). Four kept chats rest on four jobs of one family; the three-job comparison with `A4` is not a clean pair (see above). The seconds per step of every one-job cell include the engine's time to compile the named grammar of each step, the same in both arms. YaRN did not lower the S arm's exactness here (one job, 1,500 steps); that is one job. One card, one 7B quantised model, one engine and its version. The comparison cell figures are read from the printed lines and from a short cell run on the raw files, not from a second independent run.
