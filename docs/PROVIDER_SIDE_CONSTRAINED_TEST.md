## What the first run left open
Three things, in the order I care about them.
1. **The arms were not equally exact.** Exact-state (`S`) held the right state on 76.2, 78.2 and 69.2 percent of its steps at 8, 32 and 64 jobs; the kept chat (`KC`) on 100.0 percent at 8 jobs and 97.3 at 32. Lines 3 and 4 of that page read NO, and its own rule then applied: the arms' times are not compared. So that run claims no ratio of times, only what the kept chat's cache did (it worked at 8 jobs, 94.3 percent of the prompt from the cache, and gave way at 32 jobs, 6.3 percent in the second half and 8.3 times the time per step) and how small the exact-state prompt stayed (516 tokens or fewer).
2. **The kept chat at 64 jobs did not run.** The free GPU time ran out inside it, and a cell that is cut off is not saved. It carries the second half of that page's headline (lines 11 and 12).
3. **The replies were not constrained by the grammar.** That was my choice (the constrained and the unconstrained call had given the same text in 60 of 60 single tasks), I named it as a risk, and it did not carry over to 30-step jobs. The Result called the replies that were not constrained "the most likely cause" of the missing exactness and said that this was untested. This test tests it, and does not assume it.

## What changes and what does not
**Does not change** (so that the two runs can be read side by side): the model (`Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ`), the engine (vLLM, now **pinned to 0.31.0**, the version of the first run), the GPU assumed (a Colab T4), the cache fixed at **80,000 tokens** (5,000 blocks of 16), `max_model_len` 8,192, greedy decoding, at most 96 new tokens, prefix caching on, the 30-step jobs of `LONG_HORIZON_TEST.md` with episode e at seed 20261017 x 1000 + e (the first 8 episodes are the same at every K), the same prompts, policy `repair_m6` holding the state, the lockstep rounds, the stop rule at 7,600 tokens of a kept chat, the measures of the first page.
**Changes:**
* **Both arms are constrained.** Every reply must match the regular expression below, handed to the engine as a structured-output constraint; it is the same for `S` and `KC`. The expression accepts exactly the delta language of the repository's strict parser and of its byte-level grammar (`cico_parser.py`, `cico_decoder.py`): at least one item, items separated by one space or one newline, one optional separator at the end. The empty reply is not allowed (the strict parser reads an empty reply as no reply). `tests/test_provider_side_constrained.py` checks the expression against the repository's grammar and parser on random and on mutated strings, and pins the sha256 of its text, `21cdc0bf7824cff3866413dd74fc5939ccc01135e3b241dfcf9fbb1440bb0ec9`.
```
(?:ADD\[[0-9]+,[0-9]+:-?[0-9]+\]|DEL\[[0-9]+,[0-9]+\]|(?:ADD|DEL)\[\([0-9]+,[0-9]+\)->\([0-9]+,[0-9]+\):[A-Za-z0-9_]+#-?[0-9]+\])(?:[ \n](?:ADD\[[0-9]+,[0-9]+:-?[0-9]+\]|DEL\[[0-9]+,[0-9]+\]|(?:ADD|DEL)\[\([0-9]+,[0-9]+\)->\([0-9]+,[0-9]+\):[A-Za-z0-9_]+#-?[0-9]+\]))*[ \n]?
```
* **The cells** are `S` and `KC` at 8, 32 and 64 jobs, run in this order: `S` 8, `KC` 8, `S` 32, `S` 64, `KC` 32, `KC` 64 (the cheap cells first, the long ones last). The arm with the cache emptied each round (`KN`) and the random-order arm (`KR`) are not repeated; the first run's `KN` result stands as the reference for what the cache buys, and the prompts do not change.
* **A cell can be carried on.** After every round the replies so far are written to a checkpoint file (and copied to Drive); if the session ends, the next session runs the same cell again and it continues from the last finished round. The seconds of such a cell are the sum of its rounds (the time between sessions is not counted); the cell records the rounds at which it was resumed; the first round after a resume starts with a cold cache. This matters for one cell, `KC` at 64 jobs, which I expect to take three hours or more.
* **The reading lines are 17, and the headline is a quality-adjusted measure**, *exact steps per GPU hour*: 3,600 times the number of steps after which the held state equals the oracle's, over the wall seconds of the cell. A step that leaves the state wrong counts as nothing, for both arms. The reason is in the next section.

## What I read in the first run's files before writing this page
I read the six saved cell files of the first run (`docs/results/provider_side_cells/`) and, to write the adapter, vLLM's documentation page on structured outputs (it says a regular expression goes in as `SamplingParams(structured_outputs=StructuredOutputsParams(regex=...))` and that the older `guided_*` fields were removed in 0.12.0). No constrained reply from this engine has been seen; the adapter has been run only against a fake `vllm` module.
* **The refused replies.** 89 of the 4,552 replies were refused by the strict parser (80 in the `S` cells, 9 in the `KC` cells). 82 of them are exactly `DEL[r,c:v]`: a value after a delete, copied from the way a service is written in the state, for example `DEL[4,5:8]`. 2 more are a list that starts with it (`DEL[1,0:6] ADD[1,0:5]`). 5 are an add written like an edge, `ADD[(4,4):6]` (3 times) and `ADD[(3,0):4]` (2 times). After `DEL[r,c` the grammar allows a digit or `]` and nothing else, so for the 84 that start with the slip the way out is `DEL[r,c]`, which is the right delete; I expect the constraint to fix them. After `ADD[(4,4)` the grammar allows only `-`, so an add written like an edge cannot be made into the node add the model meant; I expect those to stay wrong.
* **A reply the grammar allows is not changed by it.** With greedy decoding, if every token of an unconstrained reply is allowed by the grammar, the constrained reply is the same reply. So the jobs whose first miss in the first run was a reply that parsed and was still wrong will miss again, at the same step, unless the engine's numbers differ between runs. (They did not differ inside the first run: the first 8 jobs gave the same replies and the same prompt sizes at 8, 32 and 64 jobs for `S`, and at 8 and 32 for `KC`; I compared all 8 jobs of those cells.) They are job 4 at step 18 (`ADD[1,0:6]`, refused by the gate), and, in the larger cells, jobs 17 (step 20), 20 (step 30) and 62 (step 12).
* **What that leaves, as an optimistic estimate.** If every `S` job whose first miss was the `DEL[r,c:v]` slip stays exact to the end, and every other job that missed in the first run misses again at the same step and stays behind, the exact share of `S` would be:

| cells | jobs that missed in the first run | of those, on the `DEL[r,c:v]` slip | the other jobs that missed (job, step) | exact share if the rest stays exact | first run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `S` 8 | 5 of 8 | 3 | 2 (job 3 at 16, job 4 at 18) | 88.3 % | 76.2 % |
| `S` 32 | 14 of 32 | 9 | 5 (jobs 3, 4, 17, 19, 20 at 16, 18, 20, 12, 30) | 93.9 % | 78.2 % |
| `S` 64 | 37 of 64 | 31 | 6 (the five and job 62 at 12) | 95.9 % | 69.2 % |

  This is not a prediction that the number is reached: the later steps of the jobs that slipped were never seen with an exact state, and they may go wrong for other reasons; and once a job is behind it can come back (4 of the 56 `S` jobs that missed did). It says where the ceiling is if the constraint does what I expect, and it is **below the 98 percent of the earlier long-horizon run and below the kept chat's 97 to 100 percent**. The kept chat's one lost chat (job 28 at 32 jobs, `DEL[1,0:6]` at step 1) is of the first kind, so I expect `KC` to be at about 100 percent.
* **What this means for the design.** I expect the arms to be equally exact only at 64 jobs, if at all. That is why the headline is *exact steps per GPU hour*, which does not need the arms to be equal: it asks how many correct steps a GPU hour buys. It is conservative against exact-state (a job that is wrong from step 16 on is counted as nothing for the rest of its 30 steps), and it does not depend on a threshold on exactness. The plain time ratios (lines 11, 12, 14) are still computed, and are claimed as ratios at equal exactness only if line 5 reads yes.
* **Lengths.** The longest reply of the first run was 31 tokens, so the limit of 96 new tokens cannot cut a well-formed reply. The kept-chat prompts were at most 7,556 tokens (32 jobs) and 7,154 (8 jobs); 64 jobs of 7,000 tokens are 448,000 against the cache of 80,000.
* **Times seen in the first run, on the free T4 (wall seconds around the engine's call, summed over the rounds):** `S` 37, 116 and 205 s at 8, 32 and 64 jobs (0.154, 0.121 and 0.107 s a step); `KC` 148 s at 8 jobs (0.617 s a step) and 4,902 s at 32 jobs (5.149 s a step, 1 h 22 min); `KN` 1,646 s. The ratio of the kept chat's time per step to exact-state's at 32 jobs was 42.7. That ratio was measured with the arms unequally exact; it is not a claim of this page.

## Reading rules (fixed now; the printout prints exactly these)
The seventeen lines above are the reading rules; `provider_side_constrained.verdict_constrained` computes them from the saved cells. Lines 7 to 11 use the K = 8 cells (line 7 the `S` cell, lines 8 and 9 the `KC` cell, lines 10 and 11 the `S` cell against the `KC` cell), line 3 the K = 8 and K = 64 cells of `KC`, line 12 the K = 32 cells, lines 13 and 14 the K = 64 cells, lines 15, 16 and 17 the K = 8, 32 and 64 cells; lines 1, 2, 4 and 6 use every cell that was run (line 6 the `S` cells only), line 5 every K at which both `S` and `KC` were run. Definitions as on the first page: "GPU time per step" is the wall seconds around the engine's call, summed over the rounds of the cell, divided by the steps done in it; "computed prompt tokens" are the prompt tokens minus the tokens the engine reports as cached, per step done; "the second half" is the steps from the 16th on; "the last quarter" is the last 7 of 30 steps, "the first quarter" the first 7. New: "accepted by the strict parser" is `strict_parse(text) is not None`; "exact steps per GPU hour" is 3,600 times the steps after which the held state equals the oracle's, over the wall seconds of the cell (a kept chat with no exact step at all gives an infinite ratio if exact-state had some). Thresholds as in the code: line 2: 99.5 percent parsed, no reply with 96 new tokens; line 4: 0.80; line 5: 0.05; line 6: 0.98; line 7: 1.3; line 8: 3.5; line 9: 0.80; line 10: 0.30 fewer; line 11: 0.20 less; line 12: 1.3 times; line 13: 0.50 (strictly below); line 14: 2.0 times; lines 15, 16, 17: 2, 10 and 10 times. A line that needs a cell the run does not hold reads "not run".

**The rule that decides what the Result may claim.** Line 5 reads yes: the plain time ratios (lines 11, 12, 14) may be claimed as time ratios at equal exactness. Line 5 reads NO: they are **not** claimed as equal-exactness ratios (they are printed, because nothing is to be hidden), and the claim rests on lines 15 to 17, with the exactness of both arms written next to every figure. Line 2 reads NO: neither is claimed. A resumed cell is marked in every table; its first resumed round starts with a cold cache, and the Result says how much that round differs from its neighbours.

**How the result will be worded.** In GPU seconds on the machine the run had, with the engine version and the GPU written next to them, never in dollars, never as a statement about any provider, and never as a multiple of "speed" without the exactness next to it. A "NO" on a claim line is a finding about this setup and goes into the article as such. This page makes no claim of "43 times" or of a multiple of capacity: 42.7 was a ratio of seconds per step in a run whose arms were unequally exact, and what this test measures is whether a figure like it survives.

## What this does not show
Dollars; other GPUs (the absolute seconds are a free T4's; a data-centre GPU computes prompt tokens much faster and the ratios, not the seconds, are what might carry over, which this test does not show); other models, engines or cache sizes; the cost of the structured-output backend by itself (both arms pay it, and line (c) above only compares `S` with its own first run); retries (this test sends one request a step and never asks again; a refused reply costs a retry in a real system, for either arm, and with the constraint the replies the parser refuses are gone while the ones the gate refuses are not); traffic that is not a round of K jobs; a cache that moves rows to CPU memory or disk; replies in ordinary words; jobs longer than 30 steps; **energy (never measured; only time)**; any claim that a grammar makes a reply right (it makes it well formed). It says nothing about the tensor-level roadmap.

## How it is run
Open `python/colab/axi_provider_side_constrained.ipynb` in Colab with a T4 and run the cells one at a time: it installs vLLM 0.31.0, connects Drive, fixes the output stream (a vLLM 0.31.0 start-up error in Colab), loads the model with the constraint, runs the safety cell (3 jobs of 3 steps in each arm, then the probe), then the cheap cells, then `KC` at 32 jobs, then `KC` at 64 jobs. Each finished cell is written as `axi_provider_c_<arm>_K<k>.json` and copied to Drive; the checkpoint of a cell that is still running is copied after every round. If Colab stops, reconnect, run the first cells again and the cell you were in: finished cells are skipped and the running one continues from its checkpoint. When the cells are done, the last cell puts them together and prints the table and the 17 lines. The Result goes below the line at the end of this page, and a result test will recompute every number in it from the saved files.

## Line 2: the cause, and what I do with the rule
**What happened.** At step 12 of job 19 (an `add_node` step; the oracle writes `ADD[3,0:4]`) the first run's engine had written `ADD[(3,0):4]` (in both cells in which it ran job 19), which the page counted among the 5 adds written like an edge (job 19 twice, job 3 three times). With the constraint, after `ADD[(3,0)` the grammar allows only `-`, so the reply was forced to continue as an edge, `ADD[(3,0)->(3,0):`, and the name after the colon (the grammar allows any letters, digits and underscores, with **no limit on how long**) became `4tworowsdown4colsright4tworowsdown4colsright…`, repeated until the 96 new tokens were used up. Every prefix of that reply is one the grammar allows, so the constraint held as designed; the reply was cut before its closing bracket and the strict parser refused it. Job 19 held the right state on 11 of its 30 steps (steps 1 to 11) and on none after. The same reply appears in the 32-job and the 64-job cell, as it must with greedy decoding. Every other reply of the six cells that parsed is at most 31 tokens long. The other three of the five (job 3, `ADD[(4,4):6]`, in the 8-, 32- and 64-job cells) became `ADD[(4,4)->(4,4):value#6]`: well formed, parsed, and refused by the gate.

**What the rule says, and what I do.** The page withholds both kinds of claim when line 2 reads NO ("neither is claimed"). The cause is found, above, but the rule does not say that finding it lifts the NO, and I do not take it as lifting it. So: (1) every line is printed as it read, and line 2 is NO in the table and in the summary; (2) **this Result claims neither the plain time ratios nor the exact-steps-per-hour figures.** Both are reported as what was measured, each with the exactness of both arms next to it, and the sentence at the end says what may be reported and what has to wait; (3) I checked afterwards, and this was not pre-registered, how much that one reply could matter. Counting the cut-off step as exact would change `S`'s exact steps per GPU hour at 32 jobs from 28,748 to 28,780 and at 64 jobs from 32,896 to 32,914; counting all 19 steps that job 19 got wrong as exact (an upper bound) would give 29,350 and 33,231, and the ratios against `KC` would be 40.3 and 49.8 instead of 39.5 and 49.3: lines 16 and 17 (limit 10) would still read yes, and the exact share of `S` would be 96.6 % and 98.2 %, still short of `KC`. (4) The clean way to clear line 2 is to run the three `S` cells again with a grammar that bounds the length of a name (a few minutes of GPU for the three; the `KC` cells would not change, since no reply of theirs is longer than 16 tokens and a reply the grammar allows is not changed by it). That is a new regular expression and so a new page with new predictions; I have not made it.

## What I read from it (derived afterwards; none of this was pre-registered)
1. **The kept chat's cache gave way at 64 jobs the way it did at 32, only sooner.** At 64 jobs it served 50 % of the prompt in round 1 and 86 % in round 6 (round 6 took 20 seconds), then 61 % in round 7 (55 seconds), 37 % in round 8 and 8 % in round 11; from round 11 on it served about 12,600 tokens a round, about 198 per chat, which looks like the shared instruction text and nothing of a chat's own history (an inference: I did not check which tokens they were). At 32 jobs it held up to round 12 (93 %) and gave way in round 13 (76 %). Over the second half of the steps the cache served 4.6 % of the prompt at 64 jobs, over all steps 11.8 %. A round of 64 chats took 20 seconds when everything fitted and a round of 61 chats took 732 at round 30. The cache was 80,000 tokens; 64 chats of the longest prompt seen in that cell (7,556 tokens) are 483,584 tokens.
2. **At this size the time of a kept chat is the time of the prompt tokens it has to compute again.** `KC` computed 2,246,408 prompt tokens in 4,731 seconds at 32 jobs (475 tokens a second, 2.11 milliseconds each) and 5,154,162 in 10,331 at 64 jobs (499 a second, 2.00 milliseconds); at 8 jobs, with the cache working, 323 a second. The replies are short, so with the cache gone the time is almost all prompt work.
3. **Exact-state stayed flat and small, and its time per step fell as the jobs grew in number.** Its mean prompt was 394 tokens in the first 7 steps and 332 in the last 7 at K = 8, the longest 516 tokens in any of its cells; 64 jobs of that size need at most 33,024 tokens, 41 % of the cache. It served 69 % of its prompt from the cache at 64 jobs. Seconds per step: 0.152, 0.118, 0.106.
4. **Where the exactness went.** The constraint did what the page said it would do to the format: the refused replies of `S` are 0, 1 and 1 at 8, 32 and 64 jobs (first run 6, 16, 58) and the jobs exact to the end are 6 of 8, 27 of 32 and 58 of 64 (first run 3, 18, 27). No state was ever corrupted: 0 of the 6,231 steps. **What is left is the content.** Of the six first misses, five are replies that parsed and were wrong (an add written like an edge with a made-up name, a wrong coordinate and value, a wrong coordinate, and a delete written before the add that should have stood alone, twice) and one is the cut-off loop. A grammar makes a reply well formed, not right. The jobs that miss are the same jobs at every K and answer the same way, so the exact share of `S` rising from 88.3 % to 97.2 % with K is the share of failing jobs among the first 8, 32 and 64 jobs (2 of 8, 5 of 32, 6 of 64) and not an effect of the load.
5. **The kept chat was exact at every size** (100.0 % at 8, 32 and 64 jobs, no refused reply), at the price in the table: 0.61, 4.95 and 5.39 seconds a step.
6. **The figures that go with the headline,** each with the exactness of both arms next to it: at 8 jobs `S` does 20,878 exact steps per GPU hour (88.3 % exact) and `KC` 5,923 (100.0 %), 3.5 times; at 32 jobs 28,748 (94.6 %) against 727 (100.0 %), 39.5 times; at 64 jobs 32,896 (97.2 %) against 667 (100.0 %), 49.3 times. ## What this does and does not show
* **Shows, on one Tesla T4, one 7B model with 4-bit AWQ weights, vLLM 0.31.0, a cache of 80,000 tokens, 30-step jobs and every reply constrained by one regular expression** (a description of this run, with line 2 NO beside it, and not a claim under the page's rule): the kept chat's time per step went from 0.61 (8 jobs) to 4.95 (32) and 5.39 seconds (64), exact-state's from 0.15 to 0.11; exact-state did 3.5, 39.5 and 49.3 times as many exact steps per GPU hour as the kept chat at 8, 32 and 64 jobs, while holding the right state on 88.3 %, 94.6 % and 97.2 % of its steps against 100.0 % for the kept chat; and the sensitivity above says the one cut-off reply does not move lines 16 and 17.
* **Does not show that exact-state is as exact as the kept chat** (lines 5 and 6 NO), and so no ratio of times is claimed as a ratio at equal exactness. It does not show another GPU (this one computed about 499 prompt tokens a second in the kept chat's slow cells; a data-centre GPU is much faster, and the ratios, not the seconds, are what might carry over, which this test does not show), another model, engine or cache size, dollars, **energy (never measured; only time)**, retries (this test sends one request a step and never asks again; a refused reply costs a retry in a real system, for either arm, and the one reply of `S` that was cut off would have cost one), traffic that is not a round of K jobs, jobs longer than 30 steps, or a number of agents per GPU. It does not show that a grammar that bounds the length of a name clears line 2: I have not run it.
* **What may be reported now, and what has to wait.** The figures above may be reported as the measurements of this run with line 2 NO next to them; the rule of the page does not let this Result turn them into a claim. **What waits** is a sentence of the form *with every reply constrained, exact-state did N exact steps per GPU hour against M for a kept chat, at a stated exactness for both*, which the page reserves for a run in which line 2 reads yes. It is not a claim of a speed multiple and not a claim of "as good": the quality of exact-state is lower and is written next to every number.
* **Not done and worth doing, in this order:** (1) the three `S` cells again with the length of a name bounded (clears or confirms line 2; a few minutes of GPU; a new page and a new hash); (2) retries counted, so that a refused reply costs what it costs; (3) the same cells on another GPU; (4) the GPU's power read next to the seconds.
