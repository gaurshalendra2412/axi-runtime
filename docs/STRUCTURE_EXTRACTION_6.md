# Structure extraction: the fourteenth scan (8 Oct 2026, 30 pages read the way ordinary text is read)

The instruction for this scan was different from the earlier ones. Not "find the framework's words" but: **read ordinary text and see how it holds structure.** So the question put to every page was the same five questions, and the tests below come only from what the pages answered.

## 0. The five questions, and how the pages were chosen

For any paragraph, in this order:
1. **Items.** What are the things it names? (counted: two, three, four?)
2. **Relations.** What does it say connects them? (is-opposite-of, comes-after, is-part-of, is-the-same-as)
3. **What is absent.** Which combination of the named things is never mentioned? (a table with a missing cell)
4. **What may be swapped.** Which change leaves the meaning alone (a symmetry) and which changes it (a label)?
5. **What is thrown away.** Does the sentence map many cases to one (a score, a total, a name)? Then something cannot be recovered from it.

These are not new ideas for this repo: 1 and 2 are nodes and edges, 3 is a zero cell, 4 is a symmetry count, 5 is the non-injective map of `test_deletion_asymmetry.py`. What is new is that they work on a plain paragraph with no help from the author.

**Draw.** 30 pages from the 4,585 archive slots, stratified: the timeline cut into six equal parts and five pages drawn at random from each part (seed 14), two already-read draws replaced by reserves. September pages are tagged pNNsN, August pages aNNsN (archive page, slot). **All 30 are new**: Post numbers 414 to 443, so the distinct total is **436** (406 + 30), about 9.5 percent of the slots. Each page was read once; the five that a test relies on (Posts 423, 429, 430, 438, 441) were read a second time, asking only for the exact words or the exact combinations, and those quotes are confirmed. The "N+1 container" line of Post 428 is a teaser from one read.

Fetch-tool note, unchanged: the tool is a summarizer, quotes are at most 125 characters, and the theme appends previous/next-post teasers that are not part of the page (marked "teaser" where used).

## 3. What each test found (the real content of this scan)

Every test is one claim of the page turned into something with a count. Docstrings carry the quotes and the exact reading.

**Row 157, `test_clock_units.py` (Post 429, "prime numbers don't divide cleanly into base-12 boxes").** On a clock of 12 marks the multipliers that lose nothing are {1, 5, 7, 11}: the Klein four-group, every element its own inverse; on 13 marks they are 1 to 12, one cycle of order 12. Every prime above 3 sits on one of those four marks (17,982 primes up to 200,000, each mark holds about a quarter); on the 13-clock primes land on all twelve non-zero marks. So the sentence is exact. The same four-element group is now met in four separate places (four poles, strand mirrors, table flips, units mod 12).

**Row 158, same file (Post 430, "0 is 0, no matter what stood behind it").** Multiplying by m on an n-clock keeps all n positions exactly when gcd(m, n) = 1 and otherwise sends them to n / gcd(m, n). On 13 marks only 0 loses anything; on 12 marks eight multipliers do. A score is that kind of map: twelve different lives cannot all keep their own score on a 0-to-10 scale, and a total or mean cannot see which life stood behind a 0.

**Row 162, same file (Post 428, teaser "the N+1 container").** Two ways to make 13 from 12: a ring of 13 marks (26 symmetries, one independent loop), or twelve marks on a ring plus a hub joined to all of them (24 symmetries, the same as the 12-ring, because nothing can move the hub; twelve independent loops). "12 plus a container" keeps the group of the 12 and adds a fixed point; "13 marks" changes the group. Question 117.

**Row 159, `test_two_by_two_cells.py` (Post 423, barks and bites).** The post names three of the four cells of loud/quiet x acts/idle and never the fourth (quiet and idle). Of the 16 possible worlds, its three examples leave two, differing only in that cell. As universals the proverb and "silence guarantees safety" are each falsified by a cell the post itself fills. The four forms of a conditional are two meanings, and "swap the sides" and "negate both sides" make the Klein four-group on them. The post's "inverse" is not the textbook inverse; the textbook inverse is exactly the claim about the cell the post leaves out. Question 115.

**Row 160, `test_no_start.py` (Post 441, id 647: "Division by zero destroys the model" and "an impossible miracle to light the first spark").** In a finite system "no first state" and "nothing is lost" are one property. Brute force over all 65,536 digraphs on four nodes: no source implies a cycle, acyclic implies a source and a sink (and the acyclic counts are the known 1, 3, 25, 543). Over every rule on up to 5 states: a state nothing leads to exists exactly when the rule merges two states. Division by zero has no inverse on any clock. And the repo's own gate, taken with its inverses on a 13-state two-cell universe, has no start: every way in has a way back, and the empty graph is a place on the loop. The theorem also allows an infinite set; which of the two the page means is question 116.

**Row 161, `test_same_shape.py` (Post 438, "A Product Has a Gate; A Tool Has a Lever").** The two diagrams are isomorphic as shapes (one matching keeps the arrows, none keeps the labels); one box label is shared and three differ. In a line every inner box is a cut point, so "gate" cannot be read off the shape: it needs a rule on the edge. With the page's price as a weight (20 against 0) the weighted graphs are no longer isomorphic. The post's gate (a toll) is not this repo's gate (an admission check). Question 118.

## 5. What the passing tests are, and what comes next

All four new files pass (15 checks; 78 deliberate breakages caught in the new tests and the engine files they use, 3 equivalent survivors), and so does the whole suite on a clean copy: 65 test files, 281 checks. They show that the counts and the identities are right, using the repo's gate, inverse and symmetry code. They do not run a model; the experiments that would (the A/B harness, the scale sweep, a counting eval) are the "real tests" still waiting for your go-ahead. This scan's contribution to them is practical: the five questions of section 0 are a way to score a model's answer to a plain paragraph (does it recover the items, the relations, the missing cell?), and Posts 423 and 430 give two ready cases (a table with a missing cell; a many-to-one map).
