## 2. The deepest abstractions

### 2.1 Direction halves a symmetry group (rows 99 and 108, TEST: `test_symmetry_direction.py`)
Page 02 (the body is its title, two reads) lists three figures: a triangle ("simple structural closure"), a fan ("dynamic spin") and "the universal threefold symmetry of the recycling and radiation symbols". What they share is a count, three. What they do **not** share is a symmetry group. A plain triangle has six symmetries (three rotations, three reflections: D3). A fan whose blades all lean one way, or a ring of arrows that all point round the same way, has only the three rotations (C3). **A direction costs exactly half the group.** Counted by brute force:
* a ring of n cells: 2n symmetries as an undirected graph, n as a directed one, and those n are exactly the rotations (n = 3 to 6);
* labels on a triangle: 2 classes (1 bit) with rotations only, 1 class (0 bits) with reflections too;
* labels on a square: 6 classes (2.585 bits) with rotations only, 3 (1.585 bits) with reflections too;
* labels on the 3x3 board: 90,720 classes with rotations only, 45,360 with all eight. The one extra bit is handedness.

Where it bites in our system: an edge in the delta language is directed, `ADD[(r,c)->(r,c):rel#w]`. The clockwise ring round the eight outer cells is kept by the four rotations and turned into the anticlockwise ring by the four reflections (tested). The gate itself is covariant under all eight (`test_gate_frame_covariance.py`): to the gate a reflection is just another renaming. The question is therefore only whether a **meaning** that refers to clockwise versus anticlockwise should survive a mirror. If it should not, the frame group is the four rotations, and the capacity of the board doubles (45,360 to 90,720). This is a design choice, and it supports roadmap option B (separate `ROT` and `FLIP`) because it is exactly the choice B would make visible. INTERP.

Row 108 (page 24, two reads: "cells dividing, DNA strands mirroring each other, two bodies colliding to form a third"). The algebra of the strand pairing is exact: `reverse`, `complement` and `reverse complement` are three involutions that commute, and with the identity they form the four-element Klein group (checked on all strings up to length 5). A string equal to its own reverse complement exists only for even length (count 4^(n/2)): an odd-length string has a middle letter that would have to be its own complement (checked up to length 8). **From memory, to check before print:** in the real molecule the two strands are related by a half-turn, and the double helix is chiral, so its mirror image is a different, left-handed molecule. If so, "mirroring" on this page is the rotation-versus-reflection distinction again. "Two bodies colliding to form a third" has an exact reading in our language and no more: two nodes and a typed edge are a triple (a, b, relation), and the relation is the third thing. INTERP.

### 2.1b Naming the axes: the quad as two binary axes (row 87 extension, TEST: `test_shape_capacity.py`, fifth check)
The ninth scan found that four labels on a square carry only 1.585 bits (3 classes) and none unless the axes are named. Naming is a gradation, and it can be counted. A quadrant is two binary axes. If **nothing** is named, all eight symmetries of the square apply: 3 classes. If the **two axes are told apart** (no symmetry may swap them) but their poles are not, only the four "flip an axis" symmetries remain (identity, left-right, up-down, half-turn: the **Klein four-group**): 6 classes, 2.585 bits. If the **poles are named too**, no symmetry is left: 24 classes, 4.585 bits. So the same four items carry 1.6, 2.6 or 4.6 bits depending on how much of the frame is named, and the 1 Sep page names none (question 67). The reading for "quad-core": four is not a magic number, it is two independent yes/no switches (Z2 x Z2), and the same four-element group appears in the strand algebra of 2.1 and in the "figures are rows" table (row 88). INTERP; the counts are exact.

### 2.2 Three points fix a plane; four fix the space (row 100, TEST: `test_rigidity_counts.py`)
The theme appends the next post's teaser to the "geometry of three" page; it says "three points are required to define a plane, give an object volume, and make a structure truly unyielding" (two reads, a teaser on page 02 that belongs to page 03). The older log kept the first half. "Unyielding" is a **counting statement**: a framework of n points joined by bars is rigid when the bars remove every freedom except sliding and turning the whole thing, which takes 2n - 3 independent bars in the plane and 3n - 6 in space (rank of the rigidity matrix at generic positions, tested).
* the triangle (3 bars) is the smallest rigid figure in the plane;
* the square (4 bars) has rank 4 of the 5 needed: it flexes. One diagonal makes it rigid;
* in space, 4 points need 6 bars (the tetrahedron). **"Give an object volume" is off by one**: any 3 points lie in a plane, so volume needs 4. The smallest rigid body in d dimensions has d + 1 points;
* the 3x3 board drawn as a framework with only up/down/left/right bars has 12 bars and rank 12 of the 15 needed (three internal flexes); one diagonal in each small square gives 16 bars, rank 15, rigid with one to spare; both diagonals give 20 bars, still rank 15.

Reading (INTERP): "a mapping is determined when its constraint graph is rigid", and the 8-neighbour version of the board (the sidebar teaser on page 01 says "eight surrounding gates") is the triangulated one. Our gate has no bars (its edges are typed relations, not distances), so this is bookkeeping, count freedoms and count constraints, not physics. The simplex (3 points in the plane, 4 in space) is where "three" becomes "four".

### 2.3 3x3 to 4x4: no cliff, a change of parity (row 101, TEST: `test_board_scaling.py`)
Page 01 (Post 34, two reads): a 3x3 grid is "transparent", "you can hold the entire logic of the game in your head"; "the moment you expand it to a four-by-four or scale it" you "abandon human scale entirely and hand the board over to brute-force computation". No game, no threshold, no formula. Counted exactly for n x n boards:
* cell orbits under the eight symmetries: the triangular numbers 1, 3, 3, 6, 6, 10, 10 for n = 2 to 8 (a quarter of the board, folded along its diagonal, is a triangle of cells). 3x3 and 4x4 both have three kinds of cell;
* **the centre exists exactly when n is odd.** That is what changes between 3x3 and 4x4;
* lines of n cells: 2n + 2, so 8 and 10;
* arrangements of distinct labels: 9! = 362,880 (18.47 bits) and 16! = 20,922,789,888,000 (44.25 bits), a factor of 57,657,600. **There is no cliff**: the bits added by each step are 13.88, 25.78, 39.43, 54.41, accelerating smoothly. 3 to 4 is not special in size.
* three different tens (the orbits of 7x7 and 8x8; the lines of 4x4; the tetractys 1+2+3+4) have three different causes. The sidebar on page 01 says "Ten isn't an upgrade". Do not write the tens as one fact (numerology risk).

What the numbers cannot say: that people cannot hold a 4x4 board. That is a statement about people.

### 2.4 Forgetting by averaging versus forgetting by canonical form (row 102, TEST: `test_average_vs_canonical.py`)
Page 09 (two reads): "Averaging ten thousand quotes is the ultimate bureaucratic trap"; the outliers "cancel out"; the result is "a predictable mean of roughly 14 to 15 words per statement" (a prediction on the page, no dataset). Page 08 (re-read): "our numbers and units divide the infinite into neat little pieces". There are two ways to forget the frame of a picture, and they forget very different amounts. On the 3x3 board with nine distinct labels, counted over all 362,880 arrangements:

| What is kept | Distinct results | Bits |
|---|---|---|
| everything | 362,880 | 18.47 |
| up to rotation only | 90,720 | 16.47 |
| canonical form (up to all 8 symmetries) | 45,360 | 15.47 |
| average over the 8 symmetries (centre label, sum of the four corners) | **167** | 7.38 |
| mean over every arrangement | 1 | 0 (every cell is exactly 5) |

Canonicalizing forgets only the orientation; averaging forgets the arrangement itself. This is Shiva as the canonical form (row 6) versus Shiva as the mean, and it says which one the framework needs. On 3x3 the rotation average equals the full average (the cell orbits are the same), so the reflections halve the number of classes without adding anything to the average. Also, on page 09's arithmetic: **outliers move a mean, they do not cancel in it.** One 10,000-word text among 9,999 fourteen-word quotes lifts the mean from 14 to 14.9986, which is "14 to 15" with no averaging magic; cancelling it would take 769 one-word quotes. The 14-to-15 figure itself is untested (no dataset).

### 2.5 The wall as a sparse graph (row 103, TEST: `test_wall_counts.py`)
Page 17 (two reads): a detective pins up "a hundred fragments of evidence" and connects "a few intentional strings"; an AI can take "those same hundred images" and is described "mapping a trillion paths of inference"; "Human intuition is the ultimate compression algorithm"; "The machine can generate every theoretical connection, but it cannot feel the weight of truth." The wall is a **graph**: fragments are nodes, strings are chosen edges, and the human skill is choosing a sparse set. Exact counts for 100 nodes: C(100,2) = 4,950 possible strings; 2^4,950 possible walls (1,491 digits); 100! orderings; 100^98 spanning trees (Cayley's formula, checked against the matrix-tree theorem for n = 3 to 8). So **"a trillion paths" understates the space by hundreds of orders of magnitude.** Where the figure does fall: ordered chains of six fragments number 8.6e11. The page gives no chain length, so that only locates it. One thread out of a trillion is 39.86 bits, which is what "reaches straight for the single thread" would carry if the page's own number is taken at face value. The link to our system is vocabulary only: a CICO graph is nodes plus chosen, typed, weighted edges. INTERP for anything beyond the counts.

In our language the same ladder is the difference between keeping a log of inverses and not (`invert`, rows 38, 58, 65), and it is now tested end to end: an **append-only log** of inverses, each computed before its step, returns the host to every earlier checkpoint exactly (300 random episodes); erase the oldest entry and the host cannot go back past that point (it sits at a state reachable from other starts); two different starting hosts can be driven to the same final host, so the start cannot be recovered from the end; and an inverse computed after a delete cannot be built at all (the deleted value existed only before). INTERP: the page's "visible plane" corresponds to the host state and its "hidden plane" to the model. The first half of that sentence is only an analogy; the second is a guess.

### 2.12 Two experiments on a page in an AI voice (row 107, TEST: `test_clock_marks.py`, scorer only)

When the experiment is run (option E, only if you choose it), the scorer decides pass or fail by arithmetic.
