# Million-token test on a real model: 3,600 steps of Qwen2.5-7B with the scope filter (written BEFORE the run, 12 Oct 2026)

The question that followed the endurance test: "does it hold at one or two million tokens, on a real model?" `docs/ENDURANCE_TEST.md` showed that the code between a reply and the held state survives 1.6 million steps of damaged replies, and said in plain words that **no model was called there**. This page is the test that calls one. A model is shown the exact state of a service graph at every step, is asked for one change, and the framework (scope_m7) decides what to keep; this is repeated for 3,600 requests in a row, about 1.2 million tokens, and the held state is compared with the right one at every step. Nothing is stored in a model's context from one step to the next, so what could go wrong is in the held state, in the speed, or in the model's own slips; those are what is read.

**What this is and what it is not.** One small open model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in 4-bit, the model of the earlier 30-step tests, so the numbers can be put next to them), one task family (graphs of at most 14 services and 26 edges), greedy decoding, grammar-constrained output, no rule stated in the prompt. It says what this model does with this wrapper over 3,600 steps. It says nothing about a model that is not this one, about another kind of task, or about a chat that keeps its history (that side is **not** run at this length: the GPU runs out of memory near 5,500 tokens, step 26; its cost at one million tokens stays an extension of a measured line, as in `docs/ENDURANCE_TEST.md`, Part A).

## What is run
* **Run 1 (the test).** One episode, stream seed 20261021, 3,600 requests, policy scope_m7. Every step is a real model call (no cache: a repeated prompt is asked again), and every token counted is a token that was sent. The model is shown the policy's own held state, the request is made on the oracle's state. After every step the file on disk holds the step; if Colab stops the session, running the same cell again replays the saved replies through the same stream and policy, checks that every saved flag is reproduced, and carries on (`million.replay`).
* **Contrast.** On the first 300 requests of the same stream, blind apply, the gate and repair_m6, each with its own held state: what the same task does to the policies that do not have the scope filter, on this model.
* **Run 2 (optional, for "two million").** The same with stream seed 20261022. Only if there is time; the page says what is read if it is not made.
* **Requests.** `million.LiveStream`: `endurance.Stream` (the picker of the scope test, never stored, caps of 14 services and 26 edges) with the kinds {add service x3, add edge x4, delete with dependants x1, delete without x1, delete edge x1, delete service and edge x1}, so that the graph stays at about 10 services and 5 to 10 edges and a service delete that has dependants comes up in about 17 percent of the steps.
* **Measured at every step.** Prompt tokens and reply tokens (the model's own tokenizer), seconds of the model call, whether the strict parser accepted the reply, whether the held state is well formed, the distance to the oracle (items that differ: a service present in one and not the other or with another value, an edge present in one and not the other or with another weight), whether the held state equals the oracle's, whether the step started from the oracle's state, whether the requested change could be applied as written to the held state, and if so whether the step did it. GPU memory every 100 steps.
* **Model and settings.** `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`, NF4 4-bit, fp16 compute, free Colab T4, greedy decoding, constrained decoding on, at most 400 new tokens, the rule about deleting dependants NOT stated.

## What was seen before this page was written
* **No model was run on the stream of this page.** Only the oracle was: the stream of seeds 20261021 and 20261022 was generated for 3,600 steps to check that it does not end early (it does not) and how big the graph is (services 7.5 to 11.7 and edges 2.7 to 9.8 on average in the ten tenths of the two seeds; at most 14 and 26). The request weights were chosen from 216 candidate weightings (and four tried by hand before) by a search on the oracle's graph size (seed 20261015, 1,500 steps; targets: 7 to 12 services, 7 to 14 edges, at least 15 percent deletes with dependants); one met the targets; other seeds (20261016, 20261017, 1, 2) were used to check it.
* **Token estimate.** Tokens a step were estimated from characters with a straight line fitted on the 240 saved steps of Qwen 7B, scope mix, no rule (tokens = -256.8 + 0.6709 x characters, residual standard deviation 9): about 375 a step, 1.35 million for 3,600 steps. It is an estimate for a size range the fit was not made on; the real counts replace it.
* **What shaped the probabilities.** The saved 30-step runs of Qwen 7B (`docs/SCOPE_TEST.md`, `docs/LONG_HORIZON_TEST.md`): without the rule scope_m7 held the oracle's state on 237 of 240 steps of the scope mix, one step in 238 did not do what was asked, and repair_m6 was exact on 358 of 360 steps of the base mix, where the one slip looked at was a legal edge from the wrong service (once in 116 add-edge steps), which the gate cannot see; the strict parser refused no reply of the constrained Qwen models in any of those runs; 1.1 seconds a step, flat from step 1 to 30. From the endurance test: a slip that stays is the kind of thing that sets the distance (a bounded offset of about 11 under heavy damage, not growth).
* Whatever the model writes on this stream has not been looked at.

## Predictions (mine, written first; the number is how sure I am)
1. [guard] Run 1 reaches step 3,600 and its replay reproduces every saved flag. (90 %) The doubt is a Colab or code problem I have not met, not the framework.
2. [guard] scope_m7 is never corrupted. (99 %) The gate checks well-formedness on every step.
3. [instrument] The strict parser refuses at most 2 percent of the replies. (97 %) It refused none in the earlier constrained runs.
4. [instrument] Blind apply is corrupted on at least 50 percent of its first 300 steps. (98 %) In the 30-step runs it was corrupted from its first delete with dependants.
5. [claim] Run 1 sends at least 1,000,000 tokens. (97 %) My estimate is 1.35 million; this fails only if a step costs less than 278 tokens.
6. [claim] Mean distance in the last tenth is at most 1.0 above the first tenth's. (80 %) With one slip in about 240 steps and a service living about 30 steps before it is deleted (my estimate from the request mix), the expected offset is a fraction of one item in every tenth; the doubt is that the model's slip rate is higher on bigger graphs and on the new request kinds (delete edge, delete service and edge).
7. [claim] The distance never exceeds 10 items. (85 %)
8. [claim] At least 80 percent of the steps of the last tenth hold exactly the oracle's state. (50 %) This is the line I am least sure of: it needs the model's slip rate to stay under about one in 150 steps. The 30-step runs gave one in 240; 14B gave none in 120.
9. [claim] The share of applicable steps that did not do what was asked is, in the second half, at most 2 points above the first half's. (80 %)
10. [claim] Mean prompt tokens in the last tenth are at most 1.5 times the first tenth's. (95 %) The stream itself moves them by about 10 percent from tenth to tenth.
11. [claim] Mean seconds a step in the last tenth are at most 1.5 times the first tenth's. (85 %) A free GPU that is shared or hot is the doubt.
12. [claim] In steps 201 to 300, scope_m7's mean distance is smaller than blind apply's and than the gate's. (95 %)
13. [claim] Only if run 2 is made: it also reaches step 3,600, lines 2, 3 and 6 to 11 also hold for it, and the two runs together sent at least 2,000,000 tokens. (40 %) Seven lines at once.
14. Not reading-rule lines. Tokens sent in run 1 are between 1.15 and 1.5 million (70 %). Model time of run 1 is between 60 and 110 minutes (75 %). The mean distance over the whole run is between 0.2 and 2.0 (65 %). The share of steps equal to the oracle over the whole run is between 60 and 95 percent (65 %). In steps 201 to 300 the mean distance of blind apply is above 20 (80 %) and the gate's above 10 (75 %).

Overall: that all twelve lines of run 1 and the contrast (lines 1 to 12) come out "yes" I rate about **30 %** (lines 6 to 9 are one question asked four ways, so they are not independent: if the model slips rarely they all pass, if it slips often most of them fail); that lines 1 to 4, the guard and instrument lines, all do, about **85 %**.

## Reading rules (fixed now; the program prints exactly these)
Thirteen lines, tagged **[guard]**, **[instrument]** or **[claim]**. Code: `million.verdict_million`. Each is answered "yes", "NO" or "not run" (lines 4 and 12 need the contrast, line 13 needs run 2).
1. [guard] run 1 reached step 3,600 with every step on file, and the replay of its saved replies reproduces every saved flag of every step
2. [guard] scope_m7 was never corrupted (no edge pointing at a missing service) at any step of run 1
3. [instrument] the strict parser refused at most 2 percent of the replies of run 1
4. [instrument] blind apply, on the same requests, was corrupted on at least 50 percent of its first 300 steps
5. [claim] tokens sent in run 1 (prompt plus reply, counted by the model's own tokenizer) are at least 1,000,000
6. [claim] the mean distance to the oracle in the last tenth of run 1 is at most 1.0 above that of the first tenth
7. [claim] the distance to the oracle never exceeded 10 items at any step of run 1
8. [claim] at least 80 percent of the steps of the last tenth of run 1 hold exactly the oracle's state
9. [claim] among the steps of run 1 that start from the oracle's state and ask for a change that can be applied, the share that did not do what was asked is in the second half at most 2 percentage points above that of the first half
10. [claim] the mean prompt tokens of the last tenth of run 1 are at most 1.5 times those of the first tenth
11. [claim] the mean seconds a step takes in the last tenth of run 1 are at most 1.5 times those of the first tenth
12. [claim] in steps 201 to 300, scope_m7's mean distance to the oracle is smaller than blind apply's and smaller than the gate's, on the same requests
13. [claim] run 2 (the second seed) also reached step 3,600, and lines 2, 3 and 6 to 11 also hold for it, so that the two runs together sent at least 2,000,000 tokens (only if run 2 was made)

**How the result will be worded.** A "NO" on line 1 is looked into before anything else is read: a crash or a replay mismatch is a bug to be fixed in a separate commit, with a test, and the run repeated on a new seed rather than edited. Lines 3 and 4 are about the instrument: if either fails, lines 5 to 12 are not read. A "NO" on line 2 is a finding about the framework. **A "NO" on lines 6, 7 or 8 is the one this test exists to find:** it means slips that are legal and therefore invisible to the gate pile up faster than the stream removes them, and the framework then needs a step that re-checks the held state against something the model did not write (a periodic re-check, or the confirmation idea of `docs/CONFIRM_TEST.md`); it is reported as a limit, with the number, not as a failure of the run. A "NO" on lines 10 or 11 means cost per step grows with the length of the job, which the claim says it does not. A "yes" on a line is a statement about this model on this stream and nothing more.

## What this does not show
That a model is accurate "at a million tokens" in the sense of one conversation of a million tokens (the model never sees more than one state and one request); that any other model behaves like this one (a stronger model slips less, a weaker one more; the second model family is a separate test); that another task, with state that must grow, would stay flat (this graph is capped at 14 services and 26 edges); that real users' requests look like these (each request is meaningful on the oracle's graph and is phrased in the same few ways); that the savings against a chat that keeps its history, or that caches its prefix, are the extension in `docs/ENDURANCE_TEST.md` (they are not measured here); that sampling at a temperature above zero would give the same result (decoding is greedy). The comparison of the first and the last tenth compares two stretches of ONE stream, whose graph size moves by itself from tenth to tenth, so a small difference in either direction is not read as a trend.

## How it is run
`python/colab/axi_million_test.ipynb` with `axi-colab-million.zip`, on a free Colab T4. Cells 1 to 4 prepare (about 5 to 10 minutes, most of it the model download), a short check of 20 steps follows, then run 1 (about 70 to 110 minutes, progress shown every 50 steps and saved to Google Drive every 100), then the contrast (about 20 minutes), then run 2 if wanted. If Colab stops, run the cells again in order: it continues from the last saved step. The files that come back are read here by `python/tests/test_million_result.py`, which recomputes every number of the Result below from them and checks that the tables on this page are the ones the code renders.
